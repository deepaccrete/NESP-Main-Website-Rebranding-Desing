What is hard

  • Names. The platform's settlement layer has no names, and the towns and villages layer names only 11% of sites; the DRE Atlas names 86%. Without names, developers cannot search for a place they already know.
  • Two definitions of a settlement. The two sources draw settlement boundaries differently, so building counts and connections disagree, sometimes by a lot. Records are matched to the nearest settlement within 1 km; 2,533 sites have no match.
  • Demand is modelled either way. The DRE Atlas estimates demand from buildings with its own assumptions; it is consistent across Nigeria but not field-verified.
  • The survey covers few sites. 769 communities were surveyed, mostly in 2022, in 20 states; only 761 of 17,574 sites (4%) carry one.
  • Two ID systems. The survey layer numbers its clusters separately from the off-grid settlement layer (no shared ID), so surveys are linked by location, and building counts differ for some 2026 surveys (e.g. Burari: 1,347 surveyed, 1,447 in the off-grid layer).
  • Data ownership. The World Bank data is CC BY 4.0 and can be reused with attribution, but REA cannot correct or update it. The platform's own data still has no licence set.

A possible way forward

  • Use the DRE Atlas for names, population and first-cut demand, and the platform for grid distance, mini-grids and field survey, as one site finder.
  • Add place names to the platform's settlement layer at source.
  • Prioritise new field surveys in large unserved sites, so ground truth grows where developers look.
